# Explainer script before any frame (`explainer-script.md`)

Write the words and the beat table before building a frame. A beautiful frame under a weak line is a failed piece.
For 3D build Shorts, the problem-and-fix recipe in the 3D skill's `references/shorts-storytelling.md` applies; this
file covers the 2D editorial explainer (Vox, Johnny Harris, WSJ).

## 1. Title and first line

Measured on 4,148 Shorts of one of the largest science-explainer channels (titles and views, scraped 2026-11-08; median 14M views):

- A question mark in the title had the highest median (17M) and the largest share of the top 10% (17%). Yes-no
  questions ("Could X survive Y?") came next (16M).
- "How ..." titles were the most common (1,175) but sat under the median (13M). They work when the object is
  everyday and odd (the channel's top Short, a three-word "How ..." about a body repair, has 255M).
- 3 to 6 words is the common length; over 7 words the median falls (12M).
- "You" in the title helps a little (15M). Put the viewer in it.

The first spoken line asks the title's question or states the wrong belief the video will break. No greeting, no
"in this video".

## 2. Structure (30 to 90 s)

1. **Hook (0 to 3 s):** the question, with the picture already moving on the thing asked about.
2. **Stakes (3 to 10 s):** why it matters to the viewer, one concrete number or image.
3. **Context in two steps:** the minimum background, each step one picture (a map, a chart, a document).
4. **Turn:** the surprising fact that breaks the expected answer. Hold it a beat longer than the rest.
5. **Answer:** the mechanism, shown, in at most three steps.
6. **Kicker:** a last fact or image that sends the viewer back to the start or to the comments.

Every line adds a fact, a reason or a surprise. Cut lines that only describe what is on screen.

## 3. Voice and pace

- About 2.6 to 3.0 words per second for an editorial explainer (slower than a Short's 3.4 to 3.6), so a reader can take
  in a map or a chart while it speaks.
- Write for the ear: short sentences, one number per sentence, round numbers when the exact one adds nothing.
- Every factual claim has a source listed at the top of `script.md`; check it before recording.

## 4. Beat table (`beats.md`)

One row per line: time, spoken words, the picture (mode from the SKILL table: map, chart, archival, type), the one
focal element, the camera move, the sound cue. The picture lands 4 to 8 frames before the word it shows
(`vox-psychology-and-design.md`, "Show-Before-Tell"). A beat with no new picture in 1.5 s fails the dead-air gate.
With no voiceover, the on-screen text carries the story: one short line per beat, each line read in full before the
next arrives, and a music or room-tone bed under the whole piece, since the silence gate fails without one.

## 5. Captions

Burned-in captions for vertical video: one to three words at a time, centred in the lower third above the platform
UI, synced to the voice within 0.05 s (`edit-video` for the editor steps).
